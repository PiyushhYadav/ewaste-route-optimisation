Key Concepts & Should-Know Notes

Essential algorithmic principles, complexity bounds, and routing trade-offs to know

1. Graph Modeling (What to Know)

Represented as a weighted undirected graph G = (V, E) via an Adjacency List with vertices (pickup points, road junctions, recycling centres) and non-negative road segments.

2. Asymptotic Complexity (Should Know)

  • Dijkstra's Algorithm: O((V + E) log V) per pickup node using a Min-Heap Priority Queue.
  • Greedy Allocation: O(N · M log M) to sort M candidate centres for N pickup batches.
  • Space Complexity: O(V + E) for adjacency lists and distance tables.

3. Good to Know: Local vs. Global Optimality Trade-Off

Does Greedy allocation always yield the global minimum travel distance? No. While Dijkstra guarantees the exact shortest path between any two intersections, Greedy allocation commits locally optimal choices sequentially (A → B → C). If an early pickup fills a shared facility like R2, a subsequent nearby pickup may be forced onto a longer detour to R1. Use the Compare vs. Global Optimal button in the top bar to compare both approaches live.
